Reaching milestones

    How this is calculated

    This uses the Gompertz law of mortality from 1825: after early adulthood, the yearly risk of dying grows exponentially with age, roughly doubling every 9 years. The risk at age x is a·e^(b·x), and the chance of surviving t more years is exp(−(a/b)·e^(b·x)·(e^(b·t) − 1)).

    The parameters are rough, illustrative fits to modern US death rates: a yearly risk at age 40 of about 0.25% overall, 0.18% for women and 0.32% for men, all doubling every 9.2 years. The law fits ages 30 to 90 best. It understates accidents in young adults and may overstate risk past 100.

    These are population averages. Your own odds depend on health, habits, genetics and circumstances, so treat this as a curiosity, not a forecast.
